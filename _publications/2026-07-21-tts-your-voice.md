Abstract:
Recent Text-to-Speech systems can generate highly naturalistic and personalised voices. Previous perception studies show people struggle to distinguish between real and synthetic voices of unfamiliar speakers. However, little is known about how the public perceives these synthetic voices, especially their own. In this paper, we investigate whether people can distinguish their own voice from a synthetic one. Using an iterative design approach, we created a system that clones participants’ voices on-site then collects their opinions. On average, we find that participants successfully distinguish their real voice from the synthetic one 87% of the time. While demographic factors did not predict participants’ overall identification scores, they did reveal some systematic differences in their evaluation. Qualitative analysis revealed that participants rely on factors such as pitch and language accent when determining if the voice was synthetic. Our findings provide insights for researchers and policymakers, highlighting the importance of considering public perceptions when building Text-to-Speech systems for all.
