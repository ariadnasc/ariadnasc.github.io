Abstract:
Speech disorders can affect people’s ability to communicate effectively. Previous work has shown that listeners find disordered speech more intelligible when previously exposed to the speaker, albeit with familiarisation phases of different durations. It is not yet known whether there is a reasonable duration of familiarisation that could be included in listening tests of, for example, personalised speech synthesis for Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) systems. We conducted two between-subjects case studies that exposed listeners to controlled amounts of speech for each of two speakers with dysarthric speech, prior to a transcription task. Our results show that listeners transcribe more accurately after 2.5 min of familiarisation speech, showing statistical improvement at 10 min. Results suggest that listeners make use of previously-heard linguistic cues to drive this improvement, with differences being only statistically significant for words heard during familiarisation. The familiarisation effect observed within a reasonable amount of exposure suggests that familiarisation phases could be included in listening tests of speech intelligibility and, we expect, of other evaluation dimensions such as naturalness or speaker similarity.
